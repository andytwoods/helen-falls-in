Here is a critical design review of the **Helen Falls In** concept and build plan.

---

### 1. The Steering Model

#### Flaws & Failure Modes
1. **The Jitter / Rapid-Tapping Trivialization Exploit**  
   If correction direction is re-sampled at press time, high-frequency tapping (10–15 Hz) acts as a low-pass filter on lateral velocity. If a player rapidly taps whenever they drift slightly off-centre, $t_{hold}$ never ramps up, momentum never builds, and the wobble is immediately damped.  
   *Fix:* Add a mandatory **re-fire cooldown / tap hysterisis** (e.g. 50–100 ms minimum release duration before re-sampling can occur) or make $A_0$ (initial pulse force) carry an initial impulse penalty so micro-tapping pushes Helen around.

2. **The "Wrong-Side Hold" Trap (Momentum Discontinuity)**  
   If Helen is at $x = -2\text{px}$ (left of centre) and the player holds, she accelerates right. Within 100 ms she crosses centre to $+5\text{px}$, but because direction was sampled at press time, holding *continues* to accelerate her right—towards the canal edge. The player expects holding to keep her safe, but crossing 0 while holding actively slingshots her into the wall.  
   *Assessment:* While intended as positive feedback, this feels like an input inversion bug to first-time players. If release-and-re-press is the only way to reverse direction, player input lag plus 60 Hz frame boundaries will cause accidental immediate re-presses on the wrong side of centre.

3. **Second-Order Instability (Pure Acceleration Control)**  
   Humans struggle with 2nd-order spatial control (controlling $x''$ instead of velocity $x'$ or position $x$). Combined with exponential ramp-up, the system is mathematically unstable. Without velocity damping, over-correction will diverge exponentially within 2–3 inputs.

#### Proposed Mathematical Model (Fixed Timestep $\Delta t = 1/60\text{ s}$)

**State Variables:**
- $x$: Lateral offset from path centre (px), $x \in [-W_{road}/2, +W_{road}/2]$
- $v$: Lateral velocity (px/s)
- $\theta$: Bike lean angle (rad), used for visual lean and roll torque
- $t_{hold}$: Current press duration timer (s)
- $d_{steer}$: Active direction unit vector ($-1$ for Left, $+1$ for Right, $0$ for inactive)

**Update Equations per Frame ($\Delta t$):**

1. **Input Processing:**
   - **On `Button_Down` Event:**
     - If $x < 0$, set $d_{steer} = +1$ (steer right)
     - If $x \ge 0$, set $d_{steer} = -1$ (steer left)
     - Reset $t_{hold} = 0$
   - **While `Button_Held`:**
     - $t_{hold} \leftarrow t_{hold} + \Delta t$
   - **On `Button_Up` Event:**
     - Set $d_{steer} = 0$

2. **Force & Acceleration Calculations:**
   - **Steering Force ($a_{steer}$):**
     $$a_{steer} = d_{steer} \cdot \left( A_0 + A_{ramp} \cdot t_{hold}^p \right)$$
   - **Environmental Wobble ($a_{wobble}$):**  
     Combines low-frequency drift (Perlin noise) with high-frequency bumps:
     $$a_{wobble} = \text{Perlin1D}(\text{time} \cdot f_{drift}) \cdot W_{drift} + \text{Noise}_{Gaussian}() \cdot W_{jolt}$$
   - **Velocity Damping ($a_{damp}$):**  
     Prevents infinite speed accumulation and allows tapping to act as a brake:
     $$a_{damp} = -C_{damp} \cdot v$$
   - **Net Lateral Acceleration ($a_{net}$):**
     $$a_{net} = a_{steer} + a_{wobble} + a_{damp}$$

3. **Integration & Position Update:**
   $$v_{new} = v + a_{net} \cdot \Delta t$$
   $$x_{new} = x + v_{new} \cdot \Delta t$$
   $$\theta = \arctan\left(\frac{v_{new}}{V_{forward}}\right) \quad \text{(visual bike lean angle)}$$

**Recommended Initial Constants for Testing:**
- Path Width ($W_{road}$): $80\text{ px}$ (out of $160\text{ px}$ canvas)
- Initial Steering Impulse ($A_0$): $600\text{ px/s}^2$
- Ramp Coefficient ($A_{ramp}$): $3500\text{ px/s}^3$
- Ramp Exponent ($p$): $1.8$ (quadratic-like curve)
- Velocity Damping ($C_{damp}$): $3.5\text{ s}^{-1}$
- Base Drift Force ($W_{drift}$): $250\text{ px/s}^2$
- Drift Frequency ($f_{drift}$): $0.8\text{ Hz}$

### 2. Difficulty Targets

**Is 5s newbie / 1–2min pro achievable purely by tuning?**  
Yes, but a 1–2 minute run under pure high-stress micro-tapping will cause physical finger fatigue and mental burnout. In pure reflex games (e.g. *Flappy Bird*, *Super Hexagon*), average pro runs are usually 30–45 seconds. 

#### Critical Tuning Knobs (in order of impact):
1. **$C_{damp}$ (Velocity Damping):** Controls how quickly an over-correction dissipates after releasing the button. Lower values increase floatiness and lethality.
2. **$A_{ramp}$ & Exponent $p$:** Sets the severity of panic holds.
3. **Lateral Road Margin / Narrowing ($W_{road}$):** Shrinking the track forces tighter micro-taps and reduces acceptable error margin.
4. **Grooves / Tracks ($a_{groove}$):** Longitudinal path features act as potential energy wells that catch the wheel, requiring a minimum force $A_{breakout}$ to snap out of, adding tactical variance beyond flat noise.

### 3. Build Plan Assessment

#### What’s Mis-ordered in your Plan:
- **Major Risk:** Separating *Web Prototype* from *Capacitor/Mobile Input Testing*.
- **Why this fails:** Desktop spacebar keys have low latency (~10–20ms) and crisp physical actuation. Mobile touchscreens have 50–100ms touch latency, variable touch polling, and lack physical travel feedback. 
- If you tune the steering model purely on a desktop web browser using a keyboard, **the game will feel completely broken on mobile touch surfaces**.

#### Corrected Order of Execution:
1. **Greybox Physics & Slider Engine (Web + Mobile Web):** Build the canvas physics sliders AND immediately deploy to a phone browser. Validate that the micro-hold rhythm works under touch latency before locking down tuning constants.
2. **Path Mechanics & Hazard Prototype:** Add path narrowing, grooves, and bumps to test how environmental forces interact with damping.
3. **Art & Audio Integration:** Add sprites, lean animations, sound FX, and chiptune.
4. **Native Mobile Wrapper (Capacitor):** Wrap validated mobile build, set up haptics, and handle safe areas / aspect ratios.

---

### 4. Tech Stack & Mobile Pitfalls

#### Is Pixi + Bespoke Loop a Mistake?
No, it is the correct choice. Phaser is overly bloated for a $160 \times 288$ pixel game. A 200-line fixed-timestep accumulator loop (`requestAnimationFrame` with delta accumulation) coupled with Pixi’s `Graphics`/`Sprite` rendering is lightweight and efficient.

#### Mobile / Capacitor Pitfalls:
1. **CSS Touch Actions & Gestures:**
   - Mobile browsers introduce a delay for double-tap zoom or swipe navigation unless prevented.
   - *Fix:* Apply `touch-action: none; user-select: none; -webkit-user-select: none;` on the canvas element, and call `e.preventDefault()` on `touchstart` / `touchend`.
2. **Web Audio Unlock on Mobile iOS:**
   - iOS Web Audio contexts start in a `suspended` state. Audio **will fail completely** if not explicitly resumed inside the first valid `touchstart` event handler triggered by the user.
3. **Display Refresh Rate Mismatches (60Hz vs 120Hz ProMotion):**
   - High-refresh mobile screens will fire `requestAnimationFrame` at 120Hz. If your physics loop isn't strictly decoupled using a fixed accumulator delta (e.g. updating physics in discrete $1/60\text{ s}$ chunks regardless of frame rate), physics will run twice as fast on modern devices.
4. **Touch End Dropouts:**
   - If a user drags their finger off-screen or off the canvas while holding, `touchend` might not fire, leaving `d_{steer}` stuck active. Always listen for `touchcancel` and `blur` events to reset steering states.

### 5. High-Value Design Improvements

1. **Telegraphing Steering Acceleration (Visual & Audio Feedback):**
   - Because $a_{steer}$ accelerates non-linearly, players cannot easily gauge how close they are to "point of no return" momentum.
   - *Improvement:* Make the bike lean angle $\theta$ non-linear relative to $t_{hold}$, and introduce a subtle tire skidding sound effect or visual dust particles when $t_{hold} > 0.25\text{ s}$. Giving clear perceptual warnings allows players to learn the exact threshold before momentum becomes lethal.

2. **Asymmetric/Visual Environmental Cues:**
   - Random Perlin noise drift can feel like "unexplained invisible forces" pushing the player into the water.
   - *Improvement:* Contextualize drift using visual indicators, such as wind blowing grass/leaves horizontally, puddles, or visible slope lines on the towpath. If Helen drifts hard left, let the player *see* the crosswind or path tilt causing it.
